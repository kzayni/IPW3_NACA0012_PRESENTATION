\begin{frame}{\small NACA0012 -- Ice Accretion Summary}
\scriptsize

\begin{block}{\scriptsize Verification}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Ice mass shows limited grid sensitivity, with an L1 code-to-code IQR of approximately $5\%$ for both icing conditions; horn orientation exhibits greater variability, while the 15- and 7-bin distributions generally provide comparable results.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\vspace{0.12cm}

\begin{block}{\scriptsize Condition-to-Condition Response}
\begin{itemize}
    \item AE3933 generally produces less ice than AE3932, although the predicted reduction remains substantially smaller than the approximately $15\%$ observed experimentally.
    \item A lower upper-horn angle is consistently predicted for AE3933, reproducing the experimentally observed geometric shift between the two conditions.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\vspace{0.12cm}

\begin{block}{\scriptsize Ice Geometry}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Considerable code-to-code variability remains in the predicted ice shapes, particularly in horn orientation and local geometry.
    \item The available multilayer predictions suggest improved agreement with experiment and capture additional geometric features, although the limited number of submissions prevents a conclusive assessment.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\vspace{0.12cm}

\begin{alertblock}{\scriptsize Takeaway}
The main distinction between AE3932 and AE3933 emerges in the thermodynamic response and resulting ice accretion, rather than in the upstream aerodynamics or water impingement.
\end{alertblock}

\end{frame}
